01

What is administrative law?

Administrative law governs the authority, procedures, and limits of government administrative agencies.

Big idea: Congress passes laws; administrative agencies carry out responsibilities assigned to them under those laws.

What do agencies do?

Make rules

Develop legally enforceable regulations within their delegated authority.

Investigate & enforce

Monitor compliance, investigate possible violations, and pursue authorized enforcement.

License & permit

Administer licenses and permits for regulated activities or professions.

Administer benefits

Process applications and distribute benefits under applicable law.

Sources of administrative law

Open each source to see how it shapes agency authority.

U.S. Constitution
Establishes structural and constitutional limits on government power and agency action.
Administrative Procedure Act (APA)
A federal law that establishes important procedures for agency rulemaking and adjudication, as well as standards for judicial review.
Enabling statutes
Laws that establish or authorize agencies and define the responsibilities and powers delegated to them.
Judicial decisions & common-law principles
Court decisions help interpret the laws governing agencies and resolve disputes about agency actions.
02

What powers do agencies have?

Agencies can exercise different forms of regulatory authority, subject to the laws governing them.

Rulemaking

Creating regulations that implement statutes.

Enforcement & inspections

Monitoring compliance and investigating potential violations.

Licensing

Issuing, renewing, suspending, or revoking licenses where authorized.

Adjudication

Resolving certain disputes through agency proceedings.

How does notice-and-comment rulemaking work?

Follow this simplified sequence for many federal legislative rules; particular procedures and exceptions can differ.

1
Study & research

The agency examines the issue and gathers relevant information.

2
Proposed rule published

The agency publishes a notice of proposed rulemaking in the Federal Register.

3
Public comment

Interested people and businesses can submit comments on the proposal.

4
Review, revision & final rule

The agency considers relevant comments and, if it proceeds, publishes a final rule with an explanation.

5
Compliance & possible judicial review

Covered parties must comply when the rule takes effect; qualifying challenges may be brought in court.

Business connection: Agencies may be required to consider a proposed rule’s impact on small entities under the Regulatory Flexibility Act.
What happens during an agency adjudication?
Certain disputes may be heard through administrative proceedings, sometimes before an administrative law judge. The available review and appeal steps depend on the applicable law.
How can agencies investigate compliance?
Within applicable legal limits, agencies may conduct inspections, request relevant records, or seek information related to regulatory compliance.

03

Who limits agency power?

Administrative agencies do not operate without oversight. The three branches of the federal government each have roles.

Executive branch

Presidential appointments, executive direction where authorized, and other executive oversight mechanisms.

Congress

Creates and amends statutes, conducts oversight, controls appropriations, and participates in certain appointments through Senate confirmation.

Courts

Review qualifying agency actions for consistency with governing law, constitutional requirements, and required procedures.

The agency's own authority

An agency must act within the authority Congress has lawfully delegated to it.

What does “arbitrary and capricious” mean?

One important standard of judicial review asks whether the agency engaged in reasoned decision-making. Courts may examine whether the agency considered relevant issues and explained its decision in light of the available record.

Ask yourself: Did the agency explain why it chose this course of action, or did it ignore an important problem?
04

How are agencies accountable to the public?

Transparency laws and certain legal challenges help the public monitor government decision-making.

Freedom of Information Act (FOIA)

FOIA provides a way to request records from federal agencies, subject to applicable exemptions and exclusions.

Think about it: Why might access to agency records matter to a regulated company or member of the public?

Government in the Sunshine Act
Establishes public-observation and notice requirements for covered federal agency meetings, subject to statutory exceptions.
Citizen suits
Some federal statutes authorize eligible private parties to bring specific types of suits involving compliance with or enforcement of those laws. Who may sue and for what reasons depends on the statute.

05

What about state administrative law?

State agencies regulate matters within their authority, often performing functions similar to federal agencies.

State-level authority

State agencies exercise responsibilities created by state law, including regulation, licensing, and adjudication where authorized.

Federal–state interaction

State and federal agencies may have overlapping subject areas or work together in administering regulatory programs.

Application question

A business operates in more than one state. Why might it need to consider both federal regulations and the administrative rules of each state where it operates?

Different levels of government may regulate different aspects of the same business activity, and applicable state requirements may vary by location.
