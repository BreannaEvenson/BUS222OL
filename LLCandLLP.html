Two forms that limit owner liability

LLCs and LLPs have features in common with traditional partnerships and corporations. They offer different ways to combine liability protection with flexible business organization.

LLC

Limited liability company

A hybrid entity combining partnership-style characteristics with liability protections associated with corporations. Members generally receive liability protection, and an LLC can use pass-through taxation.

LLP

Limited liability partnership

A partnership form that allows partners to gain a liability shield while retaining a partnership-based structure.

Important vocabulary: An LLC has members , who may manage the LLC themselves or appoint managers. An LLP has partners , who may organize their leadership through managing partners or an executive committee.

Limited liability companies: the rules

An LLC provides flexibility in allocating member rights and responsibilities while offering protection from many entity obligations. Open each topic to study the rules.

Formation and operating agreements

An LLC is formed by filing articles of organization with the appropriate state filing office, often in the secretary of state's corporation bureau.

Members often adopt an operating agreement addressing governance, responsibilities, and events such as death, incapacity, and dissolution. Without an agreement, state LLC law supplies default rules.

Liability protection and its limits

Members and managers generally are not personally liable for LLC debts simply because of their roles, as long as state-law conditions are met.

  • A lender may require an individual member's personal guarantee .
  • In cases of fundamental unfairness, a court may disregard LLC protection.
Taxation and capitalization

LLCs are often taxed on a pass-through basis, but members can elect corporate tax treatment if favorable.

Funding may come from debt obtained through private or commercial lenders or from sale of equity ownership in the LLC.

Member-managed vs. manager-managed LLCs

Member-managed

Management resembles a general partnership: members participate in managing and generally have authority to bind the entity.

Manager-managed

Members appoint one or more managers for day-to-day operations. Nonmanaging members are commonly investors who participate primarily in major decisions.

Managing and controlling members owe fiduciary duties to other members.

Dissociation and dissolution

Dissociation occurs when a member withdraws. Remaining members may continue the LLC or choose dissolution.

Dissolution starts the liquidation or winding-up process after a specified triggering event under the operating agreement or a decision to dissolve under applicable voting rules.

Think about it: Why might an owner choose a manager-managed LLC rather than personally participating in daily operations?

Limited liability partnerships: the rules

An LLP can begin as a general partnership and convert to a form that grants partners additional liability protection.

Formation by qualification

Under the chapter's framework, an existing general partnership obtains approval for conversion and files a statement of qualification with the appropriate public official.

What the liability shield protects—and what it does not

Partners generally are not personally responsible for the partnership's debts or another partner's liabilities simply by being partners. However, partners remain responsible for their own negligence .

Protections can differ by state: some statutes limit shields to particular negligence-related obligations rather than all contractual and other liabilities. Some states also require LLPs and individual partners to maintain liability insurance.

Tax treatment and funding

An LLP is generally a pass-through entity . It files an information return to report profits and losses, while tax obligations pass through to the partners.

Capital may come from private or commercial loans, partnership equity, or partners' financial contributions under the partnership agreement.

Partnership management

LLPs commonly operate through managing partners or an executive committee , rather than corporate officers, directors, or shareholders. A partnership agreement typically specifies decision-making structures and authority.

Connection to Chapter 13: An LLP retains partnership features but changes the liability exposure typically associated with an ordinary general partnership.
