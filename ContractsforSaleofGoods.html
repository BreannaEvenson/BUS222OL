BUS 222 • UCC Article 2

Sales Contracts Under the Uniform Commercial Code

This lesson focuses on contracts for the sale of goods governed by Article 2 of the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC). You will examine how the UCC handles agreement, open terms, acceptance, writing requirements, risk of loss, performance, breach, remedies, and international sales.

Article 2 Agreement Acceptance Statute of Frauds Title & Risk Performance Breach & Remedies International Sales

1. Introduction to Article 2 of the UCC

Article 2 of the Uniform Commercial Code governs contracts for the sale of goods. The UCC is designed to promote commercial efficiency and help business transactions move forward through standardized and more flexible rules.

What Counts as a Good?

Under the UCC, goods must be:

  • Tangible
  • Movable from place to place

Article 2 therefore focuses on transactions involving goods rather than every possible type of contract.

Who Is a Merchant?

A merchant is someone who is regularly engaged in the sale of a particular type of good.

Article 2 contains certain special rules that apply specifically when merchants are involved.

The UCC's Gap-Filler Function

Article 2 can supply missing or open contract terms when the parties have not expressly agreed otherwise.

In other words, a sales contract does not always fail simply because every possible detail was not written into the agreement.

2. Agreement in a Sales Contract: Offer

Article 2 allows a contract to arise in any manner sufficient to show agreement. Compared with stricter traditional contract rules, the UCC is more willing to recognize an agreement even when some terms are still open.

Open Terms

A sales contract may still be enforceable even when terms such as:

  • Delivery
  • Price
  • Payment

are left open.

The UCC may provide reasonable standards to fill those gaps.

Quantity Usually Matters

Quantity is generally a required term.

However, quantity may remain open in:

  • Output contracts
  • Requirements contracts
What is an output contract?
The buyer agrees to purchase all of the goods that the seller produces.
What is a requirements contract?
The buyer agrees to purchase all, or up to an agreed amount, of what the buyer needs during a particular period.

Other UCC Gap Fillers

If the parties have not agreed otherwise, the UCC may provide:

  • Delivery: at the seller's place of business at a reasonable time.
  • Payment: at the time and place where delivery occurs and in a commercially reasonable form.
  • Price: a reasonable price based on circumstances such as industry customs and market value.

Firm Offers by Merchants

A written offer by a merchant may include a promise to keep the offer open even without separate consideration for an option.

3. Agreement in Sales Contracts: Acceptance

If the offer does not specify a required method of acceptance, the UCC generally permits acceptance in any reasonable manner. The acceptance also does not always have to mirror the offer exactly.

Battle of the Forms

Businesses often exchange standardized forms such as purchase orders and acknowledgements. Those forms may contain different or additional terms.

Under the UCC, an acceptance may still create a contract even if the acceptance contains additional or different terms.

One Party Is Not a Merchant

If one of the parties is not a merchant, the contract is generally formed as originally offered.

Both Parties Are Merchants

Additional terms may automatically become part of the agreement unless:

  • The offer expressly limited acceptance to its own terms
  • The new term materially changes the agreement
  • The offeror objects to the new term in a timely manner
Modification: A UCC sales contract can be modified without additional consideration.

4. UCC Statute of Frauds

Under Article 2, a contract for the sale of goods valued at $500 or more generally must be in writing.

What Must the Writing Contain?

  1. Quantity
  2. The signature of the party against whom enforcement is sought
  3. Language reasonably showing that the parties intended to form a contract

Other terms may be established using oral agreements, prior practices, and industry standards.

Merchant Confirmation Rule

A merchant who receives a signed confirmation memorandum from another merchant may be bound by it unless the receiving merchant promptly objects.

5. Title & Allocation of Risk

The UCC contains rules governing when ownership and risk pass from seller to buyer. These rules act as gap fillers and do not replace an agreement the parties have already made about risk or title.

Shipping Contract

Unless the parties agree otherwise, sales contracts are treated as shipping contracts.

Title transfers when the seller gives the goods to the courier.

Destination Contract

In a destination contract, title passes when the goods are tendered to the buyer at the destination.

When the Buyer Picks Up the Goods

Seller Is a Merchant

Risk of loss passes when the buyer takes physical possession of the goods.

Seller Is a Nonmerchant

Risk of loss passes when the seller tenders the goods to the buyer.

6. Performance of Sales Contracts

Once the parties have formed a sales contract, Article 2 imposes duties on both sides. All parties have an obligation to act in good faith and be honest in fact.

Seller's Duties

  • Tender the goods
  • Give appropriate notice of tender
  • Take reasonable steps so the buyer can receive delivery

Buyer's Duties

Once goods are accepted, the buyer generally must make full payment at the time and place the goods are received.

Perfect Tender Rule

The seller must deliver goods that conform precisely to the contract requirements. If the goods do not conform, the buyer may have the right to reject them.

Seller's Right to Cure

If the time for performance has not expired after rejection, the seller may cure by notifying the buyer and supplying conforming goods.

If the seller reasonably believed the goods would be acceptable, the seller may receive additional time to cure even after the original time for performance has passed.

Buyer's Inspection Rights

Accept

The buyer can notify the seller that the goods are accepted.

Do Nothing

If the buyer does nothing within a reasonable time, acceptance may be presumed.

Reject

The buyer may give timely notice rejecting all or part of nonconforming goods.

Commercial Impracticability
The UCC may excuse delayed delivery or nondelivery when an unanticipated event makes performance impracticable and directly affects a basic assumption of the contract.
Installment Contracts
An installment contract allows delivery in separate lots and payment at separate times. Each installment creates a new opportunity for acceptance or rejection, although the standard for rejection is more restrictive.

7. Breach & Remedies in Sales Agreements

The UCC seeks to place the nonbreaching party in the same position that party would have occupied if the contract had been performed as agreed.

Anticipatory Repudiation

When one party clearly indicates that performance will not occur, the other party may:

  • Withdraw and sue for damages, or
  • Suspend performance until the repudiation is retracted.

Seller's Remedies

Seller remedies may apply when the buyer:

  • Rejects conforming goods
  • Wrongfully revokes acceptance
  • Fails to pay
  • Fails to meet other contractual obligations

If goods are still with the seller:

  • Stop performance
  • Resell the goods
  • Seek damages if resale is unsuccessful

If goods are already with the buyer:

  • Sue for the contract price
  • Reclaim goods where permitted
  • Seek incidental damages

Buyer's Remedies

Buyer remedies may apply when the seller:

  • Delivers nonconforming goods
  • Fails to deliver on time

Possible remedies include:

  • Rightful rejection
  • Cover
  • Money damages
  • Specific performance for unique goods
  • Revocation of acceptance in certain circumstances

What Is Cover?

The buyer may cancel the breached contract, purchase substitute goods elsewhere, and seek damages caused by the replacement purchase.

8. International Sales of Goods

International sales may be governed by the United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (UNCISG), an international counterpart to Article 2.

UNCISG

Applies to sales contracts between merchants located in U.N. signatory countries.

  • No writing is necessarily required
  • Industry practice and surrounding circumstances may establish an agreement
  • An offer needs a description of the goods, quantity, and price
  • Acceptance becomes effective when received by the offeror
  • The seller has a strong right and obligation to cure

INCO Terms

International Chamber of Commerce terms are standardized terms used in international sales contracts.

Their purpose is to reduce confusion caused by:

  • Different languages
  • Different legal systems
  • Different expectations about shipping and trade responsibilities

Chapter Summary

  • Article 2 of the UCC governs contracts for the sale of tangible, movable goods.
  • The UCC promotes commercial efficiency by allowing flexible formation rules and supplying reasonable terms when agreements contain gaps.
  • Quantity is generally required, although output and requirements contracts provide exceptions.
  • UCC acceptance may differ from the original offer, especially in transactions involving merchants.
  • Sales of goods valued at $500 or more generally fall within the UCC Statute of Frauds.
  • Shipping, destination, merchant-pickup, and nonmerchant-pickup rules affect when title and risk pass between parties.
  • The seller is generally subject to the perfect tender rule but may have an opportunity to cure.
  • Buyers may inspect, accept, or reject goods and may have remedies such as cover, money damages, specific performance, or revocation of acceptance.
  • Sellers also receive remedies when buyers reject conforming goods, fail to pay, or otherwise breach.
  • International sales may involve the UNCISG and standardized INCO terms.
