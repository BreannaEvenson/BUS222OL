01

How does environmental law affect business?

Environmental compliance can shape a company's everyday operations, long-term expenses, and legal exposure.

Operating requirements

Businesses may have to obtain permits, manage emissions, keep records, or follow waste-handling standards.

Financial responsibility

Violations or contamination can create substantial costs, including remediation and other legal liabilities.

Multiple regulators

Federal, state, and local rules may all affect a business, depending on its activities and location.

Property transactions

Environmental risks can matter when buying, selling, financing, or developing property.

Key concept: strict liability. Under some environmental laws, responsibility for cleanup costs may arise without proof that a party intended to cause pollution or acted negligently. The details depend on the law and the party's circumstances.
02

Where did environmental law come from?

Environmental protection developed from disputes over property use into a system of federal and state statutes and regulations.

Common law and nuisance

Historically, nuisance claims offered a way to address uses of property that interfered with other people's rights or enjoyment of their property.

Federal and state environmental statutes

Legislatures created more systematic programs dealing with air and water pollution, waste management, environmental review, hazardous-site cleanup, and wildlife protection.

Agency regulations and permits

Statutes authorize agencies to establish and administer detailed regulatory requirements. Permits may specify conditions under which a business may conduct regulated activities.

Why might a law aimed at preventing pollution be more useful than relying only on lawsuits after pollution has already harmed neighboring property?

03

Who enforces environmental laws?

Environmental regulation involves federal agencies, state regulators, and sometimes local authorities.

U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)

Administers and enforces many federal environmental statutes, develops rules, and oversees certain permitting and compliance programs.

State environmental agencies

Administer state laws and may implement certain federal environmental programs under applicable arrangements.

Local governments

May address environmental concerns through local ordinances and other authorized programs.

Private citizens and organizations

Some environmental statutes permit qualifying citizen suits, subject to statutory conditions.

How can an agency respond to noncompliance?

1
Monitor and investigate

Review records, conduct authorized inspections, and investigate potential violations.

2
Determine a response

Depending on the statute and circumstances, the agency may seek corrective action or other enforcement measures.

3
Require compliance

Available remedies may include administrative orders, civil penalties, or court proceedings.

What is a citizen suit?

Where a statute authorizes it, eligible members of the public may bring a lawsuit concerning particular environmental violations or failures to perform certain legally required duties. Notice and other prerequisites may apply.

04

National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA)

NEPA establishes a framework for federal agencies to consider environmental effects during decisions about covered federal actions.

Remember: NEPA primarily concerns the process of environmental review for federal decision-making. It is different from a law that directly sets an emissions limit for a private factory.

Three terms you should recognize

Categorical exclusion (CE)

A category of actions that ordinarily does not require an environmental assessment or impact statement, subject to applicable rules and circumstances.

Environmental assessment (EA)

A review used to help determine whether a more detailed environmental impact statement is needed.

Environmental impact statement (EIS)

A more detailed analysis prepared for covered actions with potentially significant environmental effects.

Public participation

Public input may be part of the environmental review process, depending on the type of review and applicable requirements.

Business connection

A proposed project involving significant federal agency action may require environmental review before a federal decision is made.

05

The Clean Air Act

The Clean Air Act is a central federal law for controlling air pollution from stationary and mobile sources.

Source of pollutionWhat it meansBusiness example
StationaryA fixed location that releases air pollutants.A manufacturing facility or power plant.
MobileA source that moves and releases air pollutants.Cars, trucks, and other motor vehicles.
National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS)

Federal standards address allowable concentrations of certain widespread air pollutants in outdoor air.

State Implementation Plans (SIPs)

States develop plans explaining how they will attain and maintain applicable federal air-quality standards, subject to EPA review.

Permits and market-based approaches

Certain sources may need permits. Some air-pollution programs also use market-based approaches, including emissions trading, to meet specific regulatory goals.

Vehicle emissions and fuel requirements

Air regulation also addresses vehicle emissions and fuel characteristics. Separate federal programs also address vehicle fuel economy.

06

Water pollution control

Federal and state laws regulate discharges into waters and protect drinking water supplies.

Clean Water Act (CWA)

Establishes a major federal framework for regulating pollution discharges into covered waters and for water-quality protection.

NPDES permits

The National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System regulates certain pollutant discharges to covered waters through permits.

Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA)

Establishes federal requirements concerning the safety of drinking water provided by public water systems.

Oil Pollution Act

Addresses oil-spill prevention, response, and certain liability and compensation matters.

Business connection: A facility that discharges regulated pollutants may have to obtain and follow the conditions of an applicable discharge permit.
Why is the Deepwater Horizon spill studied?

The 2010 Deepwater Horizon disaster demonstrates how a major oil spill can produce environmental harm, cleanup obligations, government enforcement, and civil litigation.

07

Solid waste and hazardous materials

Environmental rules address how waste is generated, handled, transported, stored, and disposed of.

Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA)

RCRA provides a framework for managing solid and hazardous waste. Its hazardous-waste program is often described as “cradle-to-grave” oversight because it follows regulated waste through stages of its management.

Universal waste

Federal rules provide a streamlined management system for certain common hazardous wastes, subject to the applicable conditions. Examples can include some batteries and lamps.

Toxic Substances Control Act (TSCA)

TSCA gives the EPA authority concerning risks associated with certain chemical substances, including information gathering and regulatory controls.

Think about an ordinary business:

Even a business that does not manufacture chemicals may need to pay attention to how it handles and disposes of regulated wastes.

08

Superfund and contaminated property

The Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA), often called Superfund, provides mechanisms for responding to releases of hazardous substances and recovering certain cleanup costs.

Two types of response

Removal action

Generally addresses a release or threatened release that calls for a more immediate or time-sensitive response.

Remedial action

Generally involves longer-term measures to address contamination.

Who might be responsible for cleanup costs?

Potentially responsible parties (PRPs) can include certain present and past owners or operators, parties that arranged for hazardous-substance disposal, and certain transporters that selected disposal sites.

Important distinction: CERCLA liability can be strict, and in some circumstances parties may face joint and several liability. Statutory defenses, liability protections, and allocation rules can affect the outcome.
Why does environmental due diligence matter?

Before purchasing a property, a buyer may investigate past uses and possible contamination. Conducting appropriate inquiries and meeting other legal conditions may matter to eligibility for certain statutory landowner protections.

What is a consent decree?

A consent decree is a court-approved agreement that can resolve certain environmental enforcement or cleanup disputes.

What is Superfund's emergency planning connection?

Superfund-related amendments and associated emergency-planning provisions helped establish chemical emergency planning and community information requirements.

Business scenario

A company finds a low-priced warehouse on property that once housed industrial operations. Why should the buyer investigate the environmental history before closing the sale?

Contamination may create substantial cleanup and transaction risks. Investigating the property's history can identify possible problems and may be important to meeting requirements for certain landowner protections.

09

Wildlife protection

Environmental regulation also protects wildlife and the ecosystems on which species depend.

Endangered Species Act (ESA)

Provides federal protections for listed endangered and threatened species and addresses certain actions affecting those species and their habitats.

Business relevance

Development, construction, or other operations may need to account for wildlife-protection requirements when protected species or habitats could be affected.
